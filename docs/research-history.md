# FlipGuard Research History

FlipGuard evolved through four distinct stages. This page preserves that lineage without presenting prototype results as current headline evidence.

## Precision-Scheduling Prototype

The initial implementation explored CKKS scale and level scheduling with a small profile space and simulated or preliminary workloads. It established the basic execution and reporting infrastructure but did not yet make decision-integrity admission the organizing research question.

## Decision-Integrity Reformulation

The project then shifted from numerical accuracy alone to threshold decision preservation. Validation, ambiguous regions, `SAFE` / `REJECTED` / `FAILED`, `NO_SAFE`, and a disjoint locked audit became explicit protocol elements. This stage also separated finite empirical certification from distribution-wide and analytical claims.

## Direct Synthesis

Catalog search became an evaluation baseline rather than the main contribution. The direct synthesizer began deriving literal CKKS parameters from supported graph facts and a decision contract, using bounded encrypted trials and predeclared failure-aware repairs. Security Policy V2 filtered the bounded catalog and established exact `Q` / `QP` object accounting.

## Multiclass Journal Extension

The later extension added a formal multiclass argmax preservation proposition, MNIST MLP-100 and FHE-compatible LeNet-5-small adapters, natural top-two-gap activation, focused paired latency, and a security-estimator reconciliation. The extension retained negative findings: no S29-over-S32 latency superiority and `PLAN_UNSUPPORTED` coverage for all seven frozen LeNet catalog profiles.

Current public claims are defined by the versioned claim registries and [Results](RESULTS.md), not by early prototype text or preliminary p5/p1 comparisons.
